What it is

Practice that behaves like a patient, not a quiz

A multiple-choice course cannot interrupt you, go quiet, or say the thing your coordinator dreads. These do.

Personas

Fourteen patients, every specialty, three difficulty levels

Each persona opens on the objection it is built around — cost, a missing spouse, a child with no symptoms, a plan that covers less than the family assumed. Harder levels do not just argue more; they hold back the information a coordinator needs to ask for.

Warmth

A live read of the room, while it is happening

The meter moves as the conversation does — up on discovery and validation, down when price lands before value. Coordinators learn to read a patient, not recite answers, because the feedback arrives during the rep rather than after it.

Scoring

Scored against your playbook, not a generic rubric

Every rep gets a 1–5 across the rubric your practice actually uses, plus the warmth read. Deliberately no single vanity number: a score that averages away the one thing they did badly is worse than no score.

Questions

What practices ask about Bella Coach

Is this a course our team watches once?

No. A course is watched; this is run. The loop is weekly — rehearse, present, review — and the personas do not get easier because someone has seen them before.

Do we need patient data to use it?

Not for Bella Coach. Role-play is entirely synthetic, which is why it is the part of Bella a practice can start with on day one, with no BAA and no consent workflow.

Who on the team is it for?

Treatment coordinators first, but anyone who presents treatment — office managers, associates stepping into case presentation, front desk covering a consult.
