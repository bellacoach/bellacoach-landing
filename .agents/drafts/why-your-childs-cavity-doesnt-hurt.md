## Why your child's cavity doesn't hurt (yet)

Your child seems completely fine. They're eating, sleeping and laughing, and the dentist is recommending treatment. It's natural to wonder whether it's really needed.

### Early cavities usually don't hurt

The outer layers of a tooth have few nerves. A cavity can grow through them without causing pain. Pain usually starts only when decay gets deep, close to the nerve in the center of the tooth.

That means **no pain often means the problem was caught early**, which is the best time to treat it.

### What can happen if it waits

Every child and every tooth is different, and your dentist is the person to ask about timing. In general, a cavity that is left to grow can need a bigger treatment later: a filling now can become a crown, or nerve treatment, if it reaches the pulp.

### Questions worth asking your dentist

- Which teeth need treatment now, and which can we watch?
- What happens if we wait six months?
- Can treatment be done in one visit or spread out?
- What will our insurance cover, and what is our part?

### Seeing it for yourself

Ask to see the photo or x-ray. Seeing the dark spot on your child's actual tooth makes the decision much easier to understand, and to explain at home.
